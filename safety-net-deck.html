Tuesday afternoon · San Francisco

Meet Margaret.

She lives on her own. She likes her tea strong and her kitchen quiet. Her window looks out toward the Golden Gate, and today a small camera is watching the room.

Safety Net is on. Gemini Live is watching her, frame by frame.

She is 81. Steady on her feet, mostly. And like a lot of people her age in this city, alone from breakfast to bedtime.

She is not unusual

1 in 4

older adults fall every year. 36,000 of them die. In San Francisco, about 30% of people over 65 live alone.

82% of falls happen with nobody else homeand 30% of those who fell lay on the floor for an hour or more.
97% who owned a pendant alarm never pressed itMost couldn't reach it. The device meant to help is the one thing they can't get to.
43% of real falls missed. Up to 85 false alarms a day.Wearables ace staged lab falls. On real ones they miss 4 in 10 and cry wolf all day. Safety Net asks before it alerts anyone.

Sources: CDC · SF DAS · Fleming & Brayne, BMJ 2008 · Bagalà et al., PLOS ONE 2012

Later that week

The kettle whistles.

Margaret turns to reach it. Nobody else is home.

Her shoe catches the folded edge of the rug.

Nobody is there to see it. Except the camera.

How it works

Three pieces, one loop.

A camera already in the roomAimed at the floor, with a speaker. Streams video and audio. No wearable.
Gemini Live APIWatches the stream, spots the fall, speaks first and runs through the safety questions: head, hip, can you move. Returns one structured event.
PhotonSends the briefing to the daughter on iMessage, relays her replies, puts her on the room speaker.
EscalationEmergency contact phoned if nobody answers. The person on the line is told to call 911 at the address. Safety Net never dials 911 itself.

Why San Francisco

Minutes on the floor, not hours.
